From Side Project to Product: Our First 100 Signals

Published August 13, 2026 · 6 min read · By DataVoxy

GoldPulse started as a Python script running on a laptop. No infrastructure, no users, no plan beyond "can I code a bot that beats random entries?" Turns out: yes. But the path from "it works on my machine" to "people rely on this daily" was messier than expected.

Here's the honest account of what happened in our first 100 signals.

Where It Started

The idea was simple: gold (XAU/USD) moves in predictable patterns during London and US sessions. RSI pullbacks in trending markets are high-probability entries. Could I automate that decision and remove emotion from the process?

Version 1 was 80 lines of Python. It pulled data from yfinance, calculated RSI, and printed "BUY" or "SELL" to the terminal. That was it. No Telegram, no dashboard, no risk management.

The Timeline

Week 1-2

Basic strategy working. RSI + trend filter. Win rate looked good in backtest (~55%). Deployed to run every 30 minutes via Task Scheduler.

Week 3-4

Added Telegram integration. First live signals sent. Immediately noticed: too many signals during Asian session (thin liquidity, random moves). Added session filter.

Week 5-6

Got stopped out three times on NFP day. Added news filter. Also added ATR-based stops instead of fixed pips. much better adaptation to volatility.

Week 7-8

Built the live dashboard. People could now see what the algorithm sees in real-time. Added the Telegram channel for public signals.

Week 9-12

Refinement. Added MACD confirmation, EMA 50 trend filter, overnight fee avoidance. Strategy became stricter. fewer signals, higher quality.

What We Got Wrong

1. Fixed stop-losses don't work for gold

Gold's volatility changes dramatically between sessions and around news events. A 10-pip stop that works at 8am gets smoked at 2pm when the US session opens. ATR-based stops solved this completely.

2. Trading the Asian session

Our first week of live signals included Asian session trades. Win rate: terrible. The spread is wider, moves are random, and reversals happen without reason. Cutting Asia improved results immediately.

3. Ignoring the economic calendar

Three consecutive stop-losses on the same day taught us this lesson. All three were during high-impact news releases. The news filter now blocks entries within 2 hours of events like NFP, FOMC, and CPI.

4. Not tracking results from day one

We started logging trades properly only after signal #30. Don't do this. Log everything from the start, even if you think the strategy isn't ready yet. Historical data is gold (pun intended).

What We Got Right

1. Building in public

Posting every signal to a public Telegram channel forces honesty. There's no hiding a bad week or pretending a loss didn't happen. This accountability makes you fix problems faster.

2. Starting with one asset

Gold only. No silver, no crypto, no forex pairs. Master one instrument first, then expand. We later added SilverPulse and CryptoPulse, but only after gold was solid.

3. Automation-first mindset

The bot doesn't need babysitting. It runs on a schedule, sends signals, tracks open trades, calculates P&L, and generates daily reports. No manual intervention needed. This is what lets a solo developer run a product.

4. Free tier everything

The entire infrastructure costs €0/month: Cloudflare Workers (free), R2 storage (free tier), Telegram bot (free), yfinance data (free). Only cost is the laptop running the script, which was needed anyway.

What's Next

The next 100 signals will focus on:

  • Publishing a full public track record with equity curve
  • Adding performance metrics (Sharpe ratio, max drawdown, win rate by session)
  • Exploring a premium tier with faster alerts and additional pairs
  • Moving execution to cloud (no more dependency on laptop uptime)

The goal isn't to become a hedge fund. It's to prove that a solo developer with Python and free tools can build a legitimate trading product that delivers value.
