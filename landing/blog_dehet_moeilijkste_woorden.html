De or Het: The 50 Most Confusing Dutch Words

By a Dutch teacher with 50+ students | August 2026 | 5 min read

If you're learning Dutch, you've probably noticed there's no easy logic for when to use "de" and when to use "het." About 75% of Dutch nouns use "de" and 25% use "het," but knowing which is which requires memorization.

After teaching Dutch to 50+ students on Preply, I've noticed that certain words trip up almost everyone. Here are the 50 words my students get wrong most often.

Rules That Actually Help

Always "het":

Diminutives (words ending in -je): het huisje, het meisje, het kopje

Infinitives used as nouns: het eten, het lopen, het zwemmen

Words starting with ge-, be-, ver-, ont- (often): het gevoel, het begin, het verschil

Languages and sports: het Nederlands, het voetbal

Compass directions: het noorden, het zuiden

Always "de":

Plural nouns: de huizen, de boeken, de kinderen

People and professions: de man, de vrouw, de leraar, de dokter

Words ending in -ing, -heid, -ij, -ie, -tie: de woning, de vrijheid, de politie

Words ending in -schap, -teit: de maatschappij, de universiteit

The Honest Truth

These rules cover maybe 30% of cases. For the rest, there is no logic. You simply have to memorize them through repetition. The good news: spaced repetition makes this much faster. Instead of reading lists, you practice and your brain focuses on the words you actually get wrong.

When in doubt, guess "de." You'll be right 75% of the time. But if you want to sound fluent, you need to know the "het" words by heart.

How Long Does It Take?

Most of my students see real improvement after 2-3 weeks of daily practice (5 minutes per day). The first 100 words are the hardest because they include almost all the common "het" words. After that, you mostly encounter "de" words and the exceptions become rarer.

The trick is consistency. Five minutes daily beats one hour weekly. Your brain consolidates the patterns between sessions, not during them.
