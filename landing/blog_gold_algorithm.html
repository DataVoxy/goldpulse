How Our Gold Trading Algorithm Works: Technical Breakdown

Published August 13, 2026 · 7 min read · By DataVoxy

Most trading signal services are black boxes. You get a "BUY" or "SELL". no idea why. We think that's backwards. If you're risking money on a signal, you deserve to know exactly how it's generated.

This post breaks down the complete logic behind GoldPulse: the indicators, entry conditions, risk management, and filters that determine every signal we send to our Telegram channel.

The Core Idea

GoldPulse is a trend-following, mean-reversion hybrid. In plain English: we trade with the overall trend, but enter on pullbacks. This avoids chasing price at the top while still riding momentum.

The algorithm runs on 4-hour candle data for XAU/USD (gold vs. US dollar) and checks for entry conditions every 30 minutes during active trading sessions.

Entry Logic: All Conditions Must Align

A signal is only generated when all of the following conditions are true simultaneously. One missing condition = no trade.

For a LONG (buy) signal:

1
Price is above the 50 EMA (confirms uptrend)
2
Price is near support level (within ATR range. a pullback)
3
RSI is below 65 (not overbought, room to run)
4
London or US session is active (liquidity)
5
No high-impact news within 2 hours (avoid spikes)
6
Not too close to market close (avoid overnight fees)

For a SHORT (sell) signal:

The mirror image: price below EMA 50, near resistance, RSI above 35 (overbought), same session and news filters.

Why so strict? Requiring all conditions to align means fewer signals. but much higher quality. We'd rather miss a trade than take a bad one. On average, this generates 2-4 signals per week.

Risk Management: ATR-Based Stops

Every trade has a pre-calculated stop-loss and take-profit based on current market volatility (ATR). This means our stops automatically widen in volatile markets and tighten in calm ones.

The Filters: What Keeps Us Out of Bad Trades

Indicators alone aren't enough. The filters are what separate a theoretical backtest from a live strategy that actually works.

1. Session Filter

Gold is most liquid during London (07:00–16:00 UTC) and US (13:00–22:00 UTC) sessions. The Asian session has thin liquidity, wider spreads, and random moves. We simply don't trade it.

2. News Filter

High-impact events like FOMC decisions, NFP releases, or CPI data can move gold $20-50 in seconds. The algorithm checks for upcoming events and blocks all entries within 2 hours of them. The keywords we scan: NFP, FOMC, Fed rate, ECB rate, CPI, GDP, and interest rate decisions.

3. Overnight Fee Filter

CFD positions held overnight incur swap fees. The algorithm avoids opening new positions after 19:00 UTC and force-closes everything before 21:00 UTC. This keeps trading costs minimal.

4. Trend Alignment

We never trade against the EMA 50 trend. If price is below the 50 EMA, we only look for shorts. If above, only longs. This single rule eliminates most losing counter-trend trades.

What About Backtesting?

The strategy was backtested across 6+ months of historical data with realistic assumptions: spread costs, overnight fees, and slippage. We optimized for consistency over maximum returns. a steady equity curve beats a volatile one.

Key backtest findings:

  • The 1:1.7 risk/reward ratio consistently outperforms tighter ratios
  • Session filtering alone eliminated ~30% of losing trades
  • The news filter prevented the largest single-trade drawdowns
  • ATR-based stops adapt better than fixed-pip stops across different volatility regimes
